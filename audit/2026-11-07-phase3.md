# Audit: Phase 3 candidates and selection (feat/model-ranking @ b08b518), 2026-11-07

Auditor: fresh-context auditor subagent. Scope: `src/model/candidates.py`, `results/model_candidates.json` (+ meta) and the
639 `p3.*` claims in `results/claims_model.json`. Spec: LOG.md "PRE-REGISTRATION, Phase 3".
Environment: `.venv` Python 3.12, pandas 3.1.1, numpy 2.5.4, scikit-learn 1.9.1, same machine as the producer.

**Verdict: the selection numbers reproduce exactly, and the pre-registered rule picks C1. The rule was applied
mechanically. 301 of 639 p3 claims are verified. 18 reproduced exactly but stay unverified because they are labelled
wrongly (seed set 0, see D2). 320 are UNCHECKED (ablations, C2/C3 secondary metrics). C1 is better than exploit-then-EPSS

## Method (independent code path)
None of the scratch scripts import `src/`, except `p3_hand.py`, which imports the producer's `add_window_features` only to compare its output with mine.

| Script | What it does |
|---|---|
| `audit/scratch/p3_rederive.py` | Starts from my own pool (`my_pool2.parquet`, built from the raw CSVs in the harness audit). It computes the 24-month assigner window with string date comparisons and recomputes the time-safe train labels per fold (numpy datetime64: added in (month end, min(month end + 365 d, cap)]). It masks the wording with its own compiled regex, fits TF-IDF and the text LR on train rows only, and builds a 5-fold stratified OOF with seed s. HGB uses the pre-registered settings with `early_stopping=False`. C3 is StandardScaler + LR(max_iter 5000) with EPSS blank = 0 plus a missing indicator. Five seed sets on both folds. Also: a C2 variant that counts every KEV addition in the window, and 10 extra label shuffles (seeds 1000..1009). |
| `audit/scratch/p3_hand.py` | Hand-checks two assigner-months with `csv` loops over the raw files. Then compares the full feature against the producer's. |
| `audit/scratch/p3_boot.py` | Point metrics and selection; bootstrap CIs on seed 0 (my own vectorised metrics, same `default_rng(0)` resample stream); paired bootstrap; unseen-assigner subset. |
| `audit/scratch/p3_extra.py` | C1 and baseline secondary metrics. An exact permutation re-run using the producer's shuffle seeds 0..19 on both folds. |
| `audit/scratch/p3_mark.py` | Writes only `status`/`verified_by` fields. A JSON-level diff confirms that only p3 status fields changed. |

**Deviations from the pre-registration:**
- **D1 (minor, disclosed in `notes.asg24`).** The C2/C3 window counts only KEV additions whose CVE was published in 2021 or later, inherited from the all-history feature. The spec says "KEV additions ... in the 24 months", with no year filter. The count differs for 19,191 of 75,148 rows. With every KEV addition counted, C2 seed-median AUC is .8376 / .8597, mean .8486 (vs .8481). **The selection does not change.** C1 still leads by more than 0.01.
- **D2 (reporting).** All bootstrap CIs, the paired bootstrap and the unseen-assigner numbers use the **seed-0** score vectors, but the headline is the seed median. In fold 2, seed 0 is C1's *best* seed (AUC .8912 vs median .8796). The claimed paired AUC gain of +.082 is therefore the seed-0 gain; the seed-median gain is +.071. CI claims say "(seed set 0)" in their text. The `paired.*` and `unseen_assigner.{auc,top500_*}` claims do not. **This cannot change the selection**, which uses only seed medians.
- **D3 (provenance).** The meta records `git_commit` cecfbc5 with `git_dirty: true`. The results were produced before `candidates.py` was committed; it first appears in b08b518. My independent re-derivation matches to 1e-16, so the values are right, but provenance is not clean.
- **D4 (pre-registration timing).** The pre-registration commit is b18032b (21:44 UTC). The results meta is dated 21:55 UTC, and LOG.md's pre-registration text is unchanged since b18032b. This is consistent with pre-registration. I cannot rule out unlogged exploratory runs (UNCHECKED).
- **Note.** The `plus_structured` ablation uses CVSS3 blank = 0 plus a missing indicator, which is the excluded "blank-score pattern" (AGENTS.md rule 4, `pool.LEAKY_COLUMNS`). It is acceptable as the one pre-registered ablation, but its higher AUC (.890 in fold 1) must never be used to adopt those features. No excluded field appears in C1, C2, C3 or any other ablation.

## 4. Leakage and reasonableness
- **Label permutation:** PASS. I re-ran the producer's 20 shuffles (seeds 0..19) exactly on both folds: fold 1 mean .5319, SD .1118, max .7342; fold 2 mean .5058, SD .1210, max .7280. All are identical to the claims. My own 10 shuffles on fold 2 (seeds 1000-1009) give mean .492, SD .097, max .597. The null is centred at 0.5, and the real AUCs (.86 / .88) exceed every shuffle. No label leak. The null is wide (SD about .1), so AUC differences of a few hundredths between variants are within noise.
- **AUC > 0.93:** no candidate run exceeds .93 (max .891). The fold-2 unseen-assigner subset AUC is **.9397** on seed 0, .930-.941 across all 5 seeds. This is not leakage: exploit-then-EPSS scores .914 on the same subset. The subset (8,201 rows, 7 positives) is easy for any signal-based score, and its bootstrap 95% CI is [.861, .991]. It is also **not** evidence that C1 handles unseen assigners well. In fold 1, the same kind of subset (13,375 rows, 9 positives) gives C1 .737 (seeds .720-.796) against exploit-then-EPSS **.920**. Only 2 subset positives make C1's top 500 in each fold, versus 3 and 2 for the baseline.
- **Base rate:** 52/35,280 = 0.147% and 32/23,367 = 0.137%, in line with about 0.15%.
- **CIs:** all 6 metrics x lo/hi for C1, C2, C3 and the 3 baselines on both folds (144 values) match my bootstrap exactly. The paired bootstrap (seed 0) also matches: fold 1 AUC diff +.0839 [-.0150, +.1814], top-500 expected diff -0.36 [-8.09, +6.0]; fold 2 AUC diff +.0823 [-.0271, +.1997], top-500 expected diff -3.6 [-8.83, +1.0].

## 5. Conclusions: C1 vs exploit-then-EPSS

| Metric | Fold 1 | Fold 2 |
|---|---|---|
| AUC | Higher in all 5 seeds (.850-.885 vs .768). The paired 95% CI crosses 0. | Higher in all 5 seeds (.854-.891 vs .809). The paired 95% CI crosses 0. |
| Top-500 | Median 22 vs 18, but the seeds range 15-22. Paired expected diff -0.36 [-8.1, +6.0]: **no difference shown**. | **Worse in every seed** (11-12 vs 14.6-15). Paired diff -3.6 [-8.8, +1.0]. |
| Work-to-catch-50% | Better in every seed (855-1,355 vs 2,253). No CI computed. | **Worse in every seed** (1,036-1,412 vs 510). |

**Supported:**
- "Under the pre-registered rule (mean AUC over two time-safe folds), C1 was selected (mean AUC .870 vs .848 for C2 and .836 for C3)."
- "C1 had a higher AUC than exploit-then-EPSS in both backtest folds and in every seed set (fold 1 .860 vs .768; fold 2 .880 vs .809, seed medians), but the paired bootstrap 95% interval for the gain includes zero in both folds."
- "At a 500-CVE budget, C1 did not beat exploit-then-EPSS: 22 vs 18 hits in fold 1 (15-22 across seeds), 11 vs 15 in fold 2. With 32-52 positives, one hit is 2-3%."
- "A label-permutation test gives AUC about 0.5 (max .73 over 20 shuffles), so no label leak was detected."

**Not supported (do not publish):**
- Any claim that C1 "beats the baselines" or "catches more exploited CVEs" without qualifying that the claim holds for AUC only.
- That C1 is better than C2 or C3 in any statistical sense, or that C1 is "best". C3 had more top-500 hits and less work-to-50% than C1 in both folds. This does not change the pre-registered selection, but the team should know it.
- That C1 "works for new/unseen assigners". The .94 is a 7-positive subset that the baseline also scores at .91. In fold 1, C1 is far worse than the baseline on that subset.
- "AUC gain +.082 (fold 2)" stated next to seed-median AUCs. Use the seed-median gain of +.071, or say "seed set 0".
- Anything about 2026 accuracy.
- Any statement that the assigner features help. The ablations are UNCHECKED, and the producer's own fold-1 drop_assigner median (.861) is at least as high as C1 (.860).
